\section{Scope}

The advisor is a ranking function, not a program generator. Asked on a
given day, it answers \emph{what is most needed}: which movements, in
which style, have been neglected relative to a target rate. How many
pieces to do, and what the session looks like, stay with the human.

It has two queries. \code{advise()} ranks candidate movements for one
part of a session (Section~\ref{sec:advise}); \code{recommend()} says
which style is most needed overall (Section~\ref{sec:recommend}).

\paragraph{What the scorer reads.} From each logged set, exactly three
fields: the date, the style, and the movement. Reps, load, distance,
duration, workout structure, free-text description and result are stored
for the human's own record and never reach the model.

\paragraph{Time.} Time is measured in whole calendar days. All rates
below are per day unless stated as per week.

\section{Axes}

\subsection{Patterns}

The set of \textbf{patterns} is
\[
\Pat = \{\mathrm{squat}, \mathrm{hinge}, \mathrm{push}, \mathrm{pull},
\mathrm{flexion}, \mathrm{brace}, \mathrm{engine}\}.
\]
Each is defined by an archetype, which is what the movement weights in
Section~\ref{sec:E} are judged against.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
pattern & archetype \\
\midrule
squat   & back squat \\
hinge   & deadlift \\
push    & strict press \\
pull    & strict pull-up \\
flexion & toes-to-bar, V-up \\
brace   & resisting movement under load: carries, planks \\
engine  & sustained monostructural output: running, rowing, biking \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{Styles}

The set of \textbf{styles} is $\Sty = \{\light, \heavy, \hhr\}$. A style
is how a movement was trained, chosen per movement when logging.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lp{0.7\textwidth}}
\toprule
style & meaning \\
\midrule
$\light$ & inside a workout, unloaded or light \\
$\heavy$ & classic strength work: fresh, long rests \\
$\hhr$   & heavy at high heart rate: roughly 60--70\% of 1RM, for reps,
           while breathing hard \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Movements and the matrix $E$}{Movements and the matrix E}}\label{sec:E}

The dictionary (\code{exercises.json}) lists the set of
\textbf{movements} $\Mov$, currently 31 plus the placeholder \code{rest}.
Each movement $x$ carries two authored attributes:

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item a row of \textbf{pattern weights} $E_{x,p} \ge 0$ with
  $\sum_{p} E_{x,p} = 1$;
\item a set of \textbf{admissible styles} $A(x) \subseteq \Sty$.
\end{itemize}

The weights \emph{split} a movement between patterns and never duplicate
it: a thruster is $E = 0.5$ squat $+ 0.5$ push, not $1$ of each. So one
logged movement always contributes total weight $1$, however many
patterns it touches. \code{rest} has an empty row ($E_{\mathrm{rest},p} =
0$ for all $p$); it lets interval work be written as movement rows and
contributes nothing.

$E$ and $A$ are authored judgment, not fitted from data.

\subsection{Cells}

\begin{definition}
Let $D \subseteq \Sty$ be the styles the user has switched off
(Section~\ref{sec:config}; normally empty). The set of \textbf{cells} is
\[
\Cell = \bigl\{ (p, s) \in \Pat \times \Sty \;:\;
\exists x \in \Mov \text{ with } E_{x,p} > 0,\; s \in A(x),\; s \notin D
\bigr\}.
\]
\end{definition}

A cell is a pattern--style combination that some movement can actually
train. The cells are \emph{derived} from the dictionary, not declared:
adding a movement can create a cell as a side effect, and the model will
then expect that cell to be trained. Of the $7 \times 3 = 21$
combinations, 16 are cells. The table gives, for each cell, the number of
movements that can serve it ($E_{x,p} > 0$ and $s \in A(x)$); a dash
means the combination is not a cell.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
pattern & $\light$ & $\heavy$ & $\hhr$ \\
\midrule
squat   & 1 & 6 & 7 \\
hinge   & 2 & 3 & 5 \\
push    & 2 & 7 & 9 \\
pull    & 2 & 2 & 3 \\
flexion & 1 & -- & -- \\
brace   & -- & 3 & 4 \\
engine  & 7 & -- & -- \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{openitem}
Two cells, squat/$\light$ (air squat) and flexion/$\light$
(toes-to-bar), are served by a single movement each. Whenever such a cell
is suggested, it is always the same movement. A test guards against
cells with \emph{no} movement; nothing guards against cells with one.
\end{openitem}

%======================================================================
\section{From the log to increments}\label{sec:increments}

\subsection{Events}

The log is a list of sets. The scorer reduces it to a set of
\textbf{events}
\[
V = \bigl\{ (t, s, x) : \text{some set of movement } x \text{ was logged
at style } s \text{ on day } t \bigr\}.
\]
Because $V$ is a set, repeats collapse: five sets of back squats on one
day are one event, and a unilateral movement logged left and right is one
event.

\subsection{Increments}

Each event feeds weight into cells. Let $\eta = \code{HHR\_CREDIT} = 0.5$.
An event $(t, s, x)$ produces, for every pattern $p$ with $E_{x,p} > 0$:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item an increment of weight $E_{x,p}$ to cell $(p, s)$ on day $t$;
\item if $s = \hhr$, a further increment of weight $\eta\, E_{x,p}$ to
  cell $(p, \heavy)$ on day $t$.
\end{enumerate}
Write $I_c$ for the list of increments $(t_i, w_i)$ received by cell $c$.
Increments addressed to a combination that is not a cell are dropped.
Two different movements trained on the same day both contribute to a
cell they share.

The second rule is the \textbf{hhr credit}: loaded conditioning partly,
but never fully, substitutes for fresh heavy work. It counts toward the
heavy cell's rate only. It does not count as having trained heavy for the
``days since this cell was last trained'' figure the advisor reports
alongside each suggestion.

%======================================================================
\section{The exponentially weighted rate}\label{sec:ewma}

\subsection{Target rates and memory}

Each style has a target rate in exposures per week,
\[
r_\light = 1, \qquad r_\heavy = 0.75, \qquad r_\hhr = 0.25,
\]
and each pattern a weight $\omega_p$, currently $1$ for every pattern.
The target rate of cell $(p, s)$ in exposures per day is
\[
\rho_{p,s} = \frac{r_s\, \omega_p}{7}.
\]
A single dimensionless constant $k = \code{MEMORY\_K} = 2$ sets each
cell's memory, its \textbf{time constant}
\[
\tau_c = \frac{k}{\rho_c}.
\]
Memory is therefore measured in units of the cell's own target interval:
a cell meant to be trained rarely remembers for longer. With all
$\omega_p = 1$:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
style & $r_s$ (per week) & target interval $1/\rho$ (days) & $\tau$ (days) \\
\midrule
$\light$ & 1.00 & 7.0  & 14.0 \\
$\heavy$ & 0.75 & 9.3  & 18.7 \\
$\hhr$   & 0.25 & 28.0 & 56.0 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{The EWMA}

\begin{definition}
The \textbf{exponentially weighted moving average} of cell $c$ on day $t$
is
\[
m_c(t) = \sum_{(t_i, w_i) \in I_c,\; t_i \le t} w_i\, e^{-(t - t_i)/\tau_c}.
\]
\end{definition}

The code computes the same quantity by the recursion
$m \leftarrow m\, e^{-\Delta/\tau} + w$ over increments in date order,
then decays from the last increment to $t$. The whole history is
replayed; there is no window. Old training matters less only because it
has decayed.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:rate}
$\hat\rho_c(t) = m_c(t)/\tau_c$ is an estimate of the cell's recent
exposure rate: under a constant flow of $\rho$ weight per day,
$m_c \to \rho\, \tau_c$, so $\hat\rho_c \to \rho$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
In continuous time,
$m(t) = \int_0^t \rho\, e^{-(t-u)/\tau}\, du = \rho\tau(1 - e^{-t/\tau})
\to \rho\tau$.
\end{proof}

In particular a cell trained exactly at its target rate settles at
$m_c = \rho_c \tau_c = k$, whatever its target. This is what puts every
cell on one scale.

%======================================================================
\section{The score}\label{sec:score}

\begin{definition}
The \textbf{score} of cell $c$ on day $t$ is
\[
\sigma_c(t) = -\log \frac{m_c(t)}{k}, \qquad \sigma_c(t) = +\infty
\text{ if } m_c(t) = 0.
\]
Positive means behind target, negative means ahead.
\end{definition}

By Proposition~\ref{prop:rate}, $m_c/k = \hat\rho_c/\rho_c$, so
\[
\boxed{\;\sigma_c = \log \frac{\rho_c}{\hat\rho_c}\;}
\]
\emph{The score is the log-ratio of target rate to recent actual rate.}
Everything below follows from this reading.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \textbf{Steady fractions.} Training a cell steadily at a fraction
  $f$ of its target gives $\sigma = -\log f$. Half the target reads
  $\log 2 \approx 0.69$ behind; double reads $0.69$ ahead.

\item \textbf{Neglect grows linearly.} With no new increments,
  $m_c(t + \Delta) = m_c(t)\, e^{-\Delta/\tau_c}$, so
  \[
  \sigma_c(t + \Delta) = \sigma_c(t) + \frac{\Delta}{\tau_c}.
  \]
  An untouched cell's score rises by $1/\tau_c$ per day, without bound:
  $0.071$ per day for $\light$, $0.054$ for $\heavy$, $0.018$ for
  $\hhr$. Growing by $\log 2$ takes $\tau_c \log 2$ days: 9.7, 12.9 and
  38.8 days respectively.

\item \textbf{Never trained.} A cell with no increments scores
  $+\infty$. There is no principled finite ceiling for ``never done'', and
  ranking and sorting handle $+\infty$ correctly.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Training exactly on target}

Real training arrives in discrete events, not as a flow. Suppose a cell
receives weight $1$ every $T = 1/\rho$ days, forever. Let
$q = e^{-T/\tau} = e^{-1/k}$, the decay over one interval.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:sawtooth}
In the periodic steady state:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\alph*)]
\item just after an exposure, $m^+ = \dfrac{1}{1-q}$; just before the
  next, $m^- = \dfrac{q}{1-q}$;
\item the time-average of $m$ over an interval is exactly $k$;
\item the score is a sawtooth rising linearly from
  $\sigma^+ = \log\bigl(k(1-q)\bigr)$ to $\sigma^+ + 1/k$, with
  time-average
  \[
  \bar\sigma = \log\bigl(k(1 - e^{-1/k})\bigr) + \frac{1}{2k}
  = \frac{1}{24k^2} + O(k^{-3}).
  \]
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(a) The fixed point of $m^+ = q\, m^+ + 1$. (b)
$\frac{1}{T}\int_0^T m^+ e^{-u/\tau}\,du = \frac{\tau}{T}\, m^+ (1 - q)
= \frac{\tau}{T} = k$. (c) Between exposures the score grows at rate
$1/\tau$ (item (ii) above) for $T$ days, a rise of $T/\tau = 1/k$; its
average is the midpoint $\sigma^+ + 1/(2k)$. The expansion follows from
$k(1 - e^{-1/k}) = 1 - \frac{1}{2k} + \frac{1}{6k^2} - \dots$
\end{proof}

At $k = 2$: the score swings between $-0.240$ just after training and
$+0.260$ just before the next session is due, and averages $+0.010$.
Training exactly on target reads ahead immediately after a session and
behind just before the next, and is on target on average. The swing has
width $1/k$; a larger $k$ narrows it at the price of slower-reacting
scores.

\section{Choosing a movement for a cell}\label{sec:choose}

\subsection{Stalest eligible movement}

The movements \textbf{eligible} for cell $(p, s)$ are
\[
X_{p,s} = \{ x \in \Mov : E_{x,p} > 0,\; s \in A(x) \}.
\]
The \textbf{staleness} of a movement on day $t$ is the number of days
since it was last logged at \emph{any} style, or $+\infty$ if never. The
cell's suggested movement is the stalest eligible one; ties are broken by
the larger $E_{x,p}$, then by the identifier (the lexicographically last
wins, which is arbitrary but deterministic). Within a cell, this is what
rotates movements; the score itself is a property of the cell, not of the
movement.

\subsection{The session rule}

Let $Q$ be the movements already picked in the current session, and
\[
L(Q) = \{ p : E_{x,p} > 0 \text{ for some } x \in Q \}
\]
the patterns they touch. Once a pattern is in $L(Q)$, \emph{no} cell of
that pattern is offered again in the session, at any style. This rule is
categorical: it excludes cells, it does not lower their scores.

%======================================================================
\section{\texorpdfstring{\code{advise()}}{advise()}: ranking a session}\label{sec:advise}

\code{advise(mode, chosen)} is called with $\mathrm{mode} = \heavy$,
which considers the $\heavy$ cells, or $\mathrm{mode} = \mathrm{workout}$,
which considers the $\light$ and $\hhr$ cells together; \code{chosen} is
$Q$. It returns, for every cell of those styles not excluded by the
session rule, the pair (stalest eligible movement, score), sorted by
score, highest first. Ties keep the order in which cells are listed:
$\light$ before $\hhr$, then pattern alphabetically.

Picking a movement does not change any score, since nothing has been
logged yet; it only adds to $Q$ and so removes that movement's patterns
from every later list. The ranking is greedy: it never looks ahead to
what a pick forecloses, because the number of pieces is the human's
choice. The Today page shows the result as three columns, $\heavy$,
$\light$ and $\hhr$.

%======================================================================
\section{\texorpdfstring{\code{recommend()}}{recommend()}: which style is most needed}\label{sec:recommend}

For each style $s$ that has cells, let
\[
M_s = \operatorname{median}\{ \sigma_c : c = (p, s) \in \Cell \}.
\]
The median rather than the mean, because a single never-trained cell
scores $+\infty$ and would decide a mean on its own.

Rank the styles by $M_s$. Let $M_1$ be the largest, $M_2$ the second
largest ($0$ if there is only one style). The verdict is:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \emph{no strong signal} if $M_1 \le 0$: every style is on or
  ahead of target, and being least ahead is not being needed;
\item \emph{no strong signal} if $M_1 = M_2 = +\infty$: nothing has been
  trained in either style's typical cell, which says nothing about which
  is more needed;
\item otherwise, with margin $\Delta = M_1 - M_2$: \emph{clear} if
  $\Delta > \log 2$, \emph{leaning} if $\Delta > \tfrac12 \log 2$, and
  \emph{no strong signal} otherwise.
\end{enumerate}

Since scores are log-ratios of rates, $\Delta > \log 2$ says the top
style's typical cell is more than twice as far below target, as a ratio,
as the second style's.

\begin{openitem}
The thresholds $\log 2$ and $\tfrac12 \log 2$ are free choices. $\log 2$
has a meaning (half the target rate), but which multiples of it count as
``clear'' and ``leaning'' is not derived.
\end{openitem}

\section{Capacity}\label{sec:capacity}

The weekly \textbf{demand} is the total target rate over all cells,
$\sum_{(p,s) \in \Cell} r_s\, \omega_p$. With 6 light cells, 5 heavy and
5 hhr:
\[
\underbrace{6 \times 1}_{\light}
+ \underbrace{5 \times 0.75}_{\heavy}
+ \underbrace{5 \times 0.25}_{\hhr}
= 6 + 3.75 + 1.25 = 11.0 \text{ per week.}
\]
Every logged movement supplies total weight $1$ to the cells of its
style (Section~\ref{sec:E}), and an $\hhr$ movement supplies a further
$\eta = 0.5$ to the heavy cells. Three sessions a week of one heavy piece
and four workout pieces is 15 pieces: 3 to the heavy cells directly, 12
to the light and hhr cells.

\begin{openitem}
The design notes give supply as ``roughly 12'' pieces a week, with no
derivation. Counted as above it is 15 plus the hhr credit. The comparison
that matters is per style: heavy demand is 3.75 a week against 3 heavy
pieces, so the heavy cells stay on target only with help from the hhr
credit, which the closed-loop simulation shows they get.
\end{openitem}

\section{Per-user settings}\label{sec:config}

Every quantity above is read from an \code{AdvisorConfig}, so each user
can be scored against their own targets. The settings page offers three
knobs, translated by \code{user\_config.py}:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
setting & effect & consequence \\
\midrule
volume multiplier $v$ & $r_s \leftarrow v\, r_s$ for every style
  & $\tau_c \leftarrow \tau_c / v$ \\
conditioning focus $c$ & $\omega_\mathrm{engine} \leftarrow c$
  & engine cells' target and memory rescale \\
disabled styles $D$ & removes those styles' cells
  & they are never suggested or compared \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

$\eta$ and $k$ are not exposed as settings.

squat/heavy & 1.4702 & 0.3077 \\

\end{tabular}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llr}

\code{advise(workout)} no longer offers hinge/$\light$ or hinge/$\hhr$:

untrained, so $M_\light = M_\heavy = M_\hhr = +\infty$, and the verdict is
\emph{no strong signal} by the second guard in
Section~\ref{sec:recommend}.

\section{Open questions}\label{sec:open}

\begin{openitem}
\textbf{No guarantee by a date.} A neglected cell's score rises by
$1/\tau$ a day without bound (Section~\ref{sec:score}), so it eventually
outranks everything. Nothing forces it to the top by any particular
day: an $\hhr$ cell sitting at $0$ takes 39 days to reach $\log 2$. This
is a deliberate bet, to be checked against real logging.
\end{openitem}

\begin{openitem}
\textbf{Start-up transient.} Every EWMA starts at zero when logging
starts, so scores read behind until about $\tau$ days of history exist:
two weeks for light cells, eight weeks for hhr cells.
Section~\ref{sec:example} shows it on squat/heavy. Nothing corrects for
it.
\end{openitem}

\begin{openitem}
\textbf{$k = 2$ is a free choice.} It sets both how fast scores react
($\tau = k/\rho$) and the width $1/k$ of the on-target sawtooth
(Proposition~\ref{prop:sawtooth}).
\end{openitem}

\begin{openitem}
\textbf{The hhr rule has exceptions.} A movement admissible at $\hhr$ is
meant to be admissible at $\heavy$ too, since $\hhr$ means a heavy
movement at reduced load. Three movements break this on purpose
(kettlebell swing, devil's press, man maker): dumbbell and kettlebell
conditioning movements have no heavy form anyone trains. It is a
guideline for authoring entries, not an invariant.
\end{openitem}

The single-movement cells (Section~\ref{sec:E}), the \code{recommend()}
thresholds (Section~\ref{sec:recommend}) and the supply estimate
(Section~\ref{sec:capacity}) are flagged where they arise.
